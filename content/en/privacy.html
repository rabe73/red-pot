Privacy

Red Pot collects no personal data. There is no provider server your data could sit on.

Draft — not yet reviewed by a lawyer. The technical statements below describe the actual architecture.

Where your data lives

Everything you put into Red Pot — shopping lists, pantry, profiles, week plans, ratings — is stored on your device and synchronised through your own iCloud account between your devices and the members of your household. The developer of this app has no access to it and cannot obtain any: no infrastructure exists through which that data would pass.

Apple's privacy policy governs the iCloud synchronisation itself. When you share a household, its data lives in the iCloud account of whoever created it.

What we do not do

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no email address.
  • No analytics, no usage statistics, no tracking, no advertising.
  • No sharing with third parties, because there is nothing to share.
  • Not a promise — a property of how the app is built.

All permissions are optional. The app remains fully usable without them; voice and scanning are shortcuts, not requirements.

No outbound connection

The app calls no service — no provider server, no product database, no third party. There is no such connection to switch off, because there is none.

What leaves the device goes to Apple and nowhere else: the sync through your own iCloud and, where your device does not dictate on-device, speech recognition. Apple's privacy policy applies to both.

Recipe suggestions are made on the device

The recipe and week-planning features work from a body of recipe components shipped with the app. Nothing is searched for on the web and nothing is sent to a language model in the cloud. What the app learns about your preferences stays on your devices and within the household you already share with — the developer sees none of it.

Taking your data with you, or deleting it

You can export a household to a file and import it again. You can delete at any time in the app; since nothing is held anywhere else, that deletes everything there is to delete — apart from the copies in other household members' iCloud accounts, which they delete themselves.

Your rights

You have the rights set out in Articles 15 to 21 GDPR — access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection. In practice they have little to bite on here, and that is in your favour: we hold no data about you to disclose or delete. Your data sits in your own iCloud and in the app on your device, both under your control rather than ours. Export and deletion happen in the app itself.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection supervisory authority. The competent one here is the Bayerisches Landesamt für Datenschutzaufsicht (BayLDA), Ansbach.
